# Gate G1: do frames help open-ended EduVidQA answers?

**Answer: no; evenly placed frames make answers worse.** On 74 questions from 20 EduVidQA
official-training videos (timestamp given), adding 4 frames to the transcript lowers both FactQA
precision and recall by about 10 points, with 95% intervals below zero.

## Setup
- Questions: 92 drawn in a fixed hash order from 20 training videos (at most 5 per video). 74 have
  both answers judged; 18 were left unscored when the free judge endpoint was overloaded.
  The 269 expert-verified real questions were not used.
- Answerer: frozen Qwen3-VL-2B with the v3 open-ended prompt (256 tokens).
- Conditions, identical text: **T** = transcript lines within ±120 s of the question timestamp;
  **TF** = T + 4 frames evenly spaced in [t − 30 s, t + 10 s].
- Judge: FactQA (paper prompt, verbatim) with `nvidia/nemotron-3-ultra-550b-a55b` on NVIDIA NIM's
  free tier; 338 verdicts, none unparsable. Text metrics as in the paper; entailment with
  `cross-encoder/nli-roberta-base`.

## Results (TF − T, 95% CI by video bootstrap, 74 questions)

| Metric | T | TF | TF − T |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FactQA precision** (accuracy) | 0.587 | 0.493 | **−0.094** (−0.182 to −0.001) |
| **FactQA recall** (coverage) | 0.501 | 0.403 | **−0.098** (−0.186 to −0.009) |
| BLEU-1 | 0.407 | 0.366 | −0.041 (−0.076 to −0.014) |
| METEOR | 0.311 | 0.283 | −0.027 (−0.048 to −0.009) |
| ROUGE-L | 0.270 | 0.260 | −0.010 (−0.024 to +0.004) |
| Entailment | 0.153 | 0.230 | +0.077 (−0.007 to +0.164) |
| Answer length (words) | 111 | 105 | −5.4 (−12.5 to +1.8) |

Per question (mean of FactQA precision and recall, ±0.05 counted as equal): frames better in 24,
equal in 12, worse in 38. Choosing the better condition per question (an oracle that sees the
scores) would give 0.601 vs 0.544 for transcript only; this is an optimistic upper bound because it
picks the larger of two noisy measurements.

## What this does and does not show
- Shown: for this answerer, these questions and **naive frame placement**, frames hurt on average.
- Likely reasons (not tested): the synthetic training questions were generated by GPT-4o *from the
  NPTEL transcripts*, so the transcript is designed to contain the answer; evenly placed frames
  around a timestamp with ~35 s error often show the lecturer or an unrelated slide, which can
  distract a 2B model.
- Not shown: that *well-selected* frames cannot help, or that the 269 expert-written real questions
  behave the same way (the paper reports similar rates of visual wording, 44% vs 46%).
- Judge caveat: one judge model; a Gemini 3.8 Flash cross-check on a sample is still pending.
